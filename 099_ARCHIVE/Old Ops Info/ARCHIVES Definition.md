> **The ~🔴PARA~ Method**
> ****~🔴A~ = ~🔴ARCHIVES~** in the ***PARA Method: A Simple System for Organizing Your Digital Life***. Learn more at [**==🔴Forte Labs==**](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/).
>
> Archives are "inactive items from the other three categories." I differentiate archive items from trash by asking the question, "Could this ever possibly be useful again, for any purpose?" If yes, archive it. If no, trash it.

## What types of materials go in Archives?

- **Documentation for deprecated systems and/or groupings of content**. Old brand materials, old code, and old content collections should be archived for a year, give or take, before being considered for the trash bin. If it’s part of a system, it will likely need to be referenced for some reason, even if no longer active. 
- **Superseded content that still needs a record maintained**. I find myself needing paper trails more often than I’d think, sometimes just because it’s the only way to find a clue about another piece of content. This applies especially to content with legal ramifications, like outdated privacy policies and terms.

## What materials can go straight to the trash?

- I’m sure plenty disagree, but I maintain that **not much should go straight to the trash**. If there’s any possibility of needing it for reference, there’s no harm in storing content in the Archives folder for a while. The main exception would be space limitations, but that’s hardly a concern with most apps and platforms today.
  - That said, if you’re going to err on the side of archiving over trash, you should have some sort of organization system in place. You don’t want to just throw everything in the Archives folder as is. That gets messy fast.
- Immediately canceled drafts and other **materials that haven’t had any time to "take root"** among your files can usually go in the trash right away. Even one or two links can turn into a many-tentacled monster within a few days or so, so the key question is: Am I sure there are no connections to anything else?
