Notes and ideas on how to sell myself (hey, not like that!), particularly important when I’m trying to land a job.

## What I offer

In a typical work day, I might:

- Chat with the product owners and dev leads to finalize requirements for next quarter’s big launch.
- Create a ticket with all the details. And by “all” I mean design and tech specs so clear and precise it’ll make an *engineer* crack half a smile.
- Then head over to marketing to talk about showing up in the AI results boxes.
- I think I’ll also drop in just to reassure those “passionate” stakeholders the launch is looking good.
- Wrap up the day by documenting the heck out of this new widget so everybody knows what’s going on, and not just for the launch. Five years from now, somebody will have a question about why this thing was built this way, and they’ll know exactly where to find the answer.

And so it goes day to day, people talking to each other and to machines. The people don’t have to have all the answers. They just need to know where to find them. Come to think of it, that applies to the machines too. The knowledge-mangement *system* is the secret sauce.

Which nodes need to connect, what do they need to tell each other, and how far out do we need to extrapolate for potential impacts and our cotingency plans? These are the questions that increasingly need to be asked, explicitly, even for everyday workflows.

While we’re on the subject, I’ll schedule some time for us to talk about what things are gonna look like a few years from now. I know I know, it seems tedious and maybe even painful. But man oh man we’re gonna thank ourselves for putting in the work.
