# What is the ~🔴PARA~ Method?

> [!INFO]
> One of the most popular systems for organizing life online, the [**==🔴PARA Method==**](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/) separates work and materials into **~🔴P~rojects**, **~🔴A~reas**, **~🔴R~esources**, and **~🔴A~rchives**.

It's not perfect, but neither is any other system, and PARA is easy to get started and stick with the program. Plus, it can be modified to suit most individuals' needs. On that note, I've used this note to explain PARA in my own words, including modifications that suit my ways of working.

---

## Explanation of the PARA Method

### What is the PARA Method?

The **PARA Method**, developed by **Tiago Forte**, stands for Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives. This organizational framework helps individuals effectively manage their digital information by categorizing it into four distinct categories:

- **~🔴P~rojects**: Ongoing tasks or goals that have a specific deadline. Each project has a clear outcome and requires active management.
- **~🔴A~reas**: These are spheres of activity with a standard to maintain over time, like health, work, or personal development. They do not have a specific deadline but are essential for ongoing well-being.
- **~🔴R~esources**: This category includes all materials and references that might be useful for both projects and areas. It’s more of a knowledge repository.
- **~🔴A~rchives**: Completed projects and inactive materials go here, ensuring they are stored but not cluttering current workflows.

### Advantages of the PARA Method

1. **Simplicity**: The framework is easy to understand and implement, making it accessible for individuals unfamiliar with complex organizational systems.
2. **Flexibility**: Users can adapt the method to fit their unique needs, accommodating various workflows and preferences.
3. **Focus**: By separating active projects from ongoing areas and resources, it fosters clarity and focus.

## Notes on my setup

I like to number the top-level folders, particularly when the app I'm using doesn't have options for sorting folders. Almost without exception, all such apps will sort A → Z, so I number how I want the folders to sort:

- Projects, Areas, Resources in spots 1, 2, and 3, respectively.
- Archive is always the last folder, a personal preference all the way.
- Inbox, if added manually, is in the "zero" position to denote that it's a primer.
- Any other folders (see "**098_Sandbox**") are added before Archive, as they are part of the main flow, and they feed into Archive.

This is the best way I've found to appease my organizational quirks and make sure the PARA folders are always labeled the exact same way, across all apps and instances.
